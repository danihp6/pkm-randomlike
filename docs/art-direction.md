# PokeRandomLike — Art Direction

## Medium

GBA-era Pokémon routes: 24x24 tiles, 32x32 overworld characters, one screen per
route, camera follows the player, tall grass that moves when you cross it.
Battle scenes are the classic two-platform framing with animated bars, not
full-screen illustrations.

## Palette

Tilesets carry their own palette, declared per biome in `src/data/tilesets/*.ts`
and used as the fallback fill when an atlas has not loaded. Background
`#10161f`, UI text `#f8fafc` on `#000000aa`.

The drawn UI chrome carries its own: one **dark glass card**, the `#10161f`
surface at 0.72 alpha (the battle's message box, and only it, at 0.80) under a
1px `rgba(255,255,255,0.16)` hairline, a 1px `rgba(0,0,0,0.35)` shadow ring on
the card's own edge, an inset top highlight `rgba(255,255,255,0.14)` and an 11px
corner on both skins. Its ink is the light `#f8fafc` (`FRLG_INK`), its secondary
tone `#94a3b8` and its one accent `#7dd3fc`, reserved for selection and controls.

## Provenance — read before adding art

Three tiers, in order of preference:

1. **Procedural / CC0** — `src/assets/tileset.png` is generated by
   `scripts/legacy-art/generate-tileset.mjs`. Safe to ship, preferred for
   anything public. The UI chrome joins this tier: `src/assets/ui/window.png`,
   `textbox.png` and `font.png` are **drawn** by `scripts/ui/build-chrome.mjs`
   (`npm run data:ui`) from the committed tree, so the interface — panels, the
   battle message box, the HUD and the type — ships even though the FRLG rips it
   derives its shape from do not (`O4`).
2. **Imported game rips (gitignored, never committed)** — `wood.png`,
   `desert.png`, `apple-woods.png`, `lush-prairie.png`, `beach-cave.png`,
   `items.png`, `moves.png`, `hud.png`, `pokemon/*.png`, the FRLG UI art
   (`frlg/item-icons.png`, `frlg/window.png`, `frlg/textbox.png` and
   `frlg/font.png` — the window chrome and the font are superseded by the drawn
   `ui/**` set, see the UI section below — `frlg/hpbar.png`, `frlg/cursor.png`,
   `frlg/trainers.png`,
   `frlg/overworld.png`, `frlg/player.png`), the packed `audio/*.wav` and the
   battle sprites in `public/anil/`. The PMD ones
   come from Pokémon Mystery Dungeon rips (PMDCollab and spriters-resource) via the
   sibling project `pkm-mystery-dungeon-like`; the overworld set is now packed
   straight from **PMDCollab's SpriteCollab** project (see the PMD Collab section
   below); the FRLG ones come from
   spriters-resource and the pret/pokefirered decomp (see the FRLG sections below);
   the battle sprites come from the fangame Pokémon Añil (see the Añil section
   below). All are Nintendo/GameFreak/Chunsoft art, as the user asked. **Never
   redistribute**: the source trees live in `sprite-sources/` (or the private
   `pkrl-assets` repository), every packed output is listed in `.gitignore`, and
   no crop or cue enters the repository or its history. A clone without the store
   builds and boots against the committed blanks in
   `src/assets/placeholders/` (`npm run assets:pack` restores the real art).
   Tileset rips are credited upstream to SilverDeoxys563 (spriters-resource).
3. **Third-party CC0** — if a pack is added, record its licence and author in
   this file and keep the source pack in `sprite-sources/` (gitignored).

## FireRed/LeafGreen sources (downloaded, local only)

For a GBA-native route look, a set of FRLG sheets lives in
`sprite-sources/frlg/` (gitignored, never redistributed). Downloaded from
spriters-resource:

| File | Size | Contents | Credit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tileset-2.png` | 477x800 | outdoor terrain, native 16x16 tiles | ripped by fabnt |
| `tileset.png` | 300x450 | mixed terrain/building compilation, magenta-keyed | ripped by Hoeloo |
| `tileset-1.png` | 1096x1090 | buildings compendium (not autotile-ready) | ripped by fabnt |
| `npc-overworld.png` | 238x2967 | overworld characters: 4-facing walk frames | spriters-resource |
| `trainers.png` | 536x1400 | battle trainer sprites | spriters-resource |
| `player-sprites.png` | 673x638 | player overworld frames | spriters-resource |

These tiles are 16x16, so any atlas built from them declares `tileSize: 16`
(`D18`) instead of the 24px of the PMD atlases. `scripts/frlg/build-tilesets.mjs`
composes three field atlases — outdoor, forest and cave — into the 64x208
contract: the floor and its variants, the wall as a repeating whole-object motif
(a three-wide tree that stacks vertically — a canopy at the top of a mass, solid
canopy inside, a trunk at its foot — composited over the floor), the sheet's
shared rocky-banked pond, and the cliff lip on the sheet's last cell for the
route's one-way ledges (`FRAME_LEDGE`, `D26`). The motif is what makes a tree
border read as complete trees instead of one repeated stamp; the route's shape
follows the same biome (`docs/routes-spec.md` §2), so a forest reads like Viridian
Forest and a cave like Mt. Moon. They are registered as tilesets and biomes
(`src/data/tilesets/frlg.ts`, `src/data/biomes.ts`) and are the only fields the
route chain cycles, so a run never falls back to the imported PMD sheets; a beach
field is the remaining open one.

Battle trainer sprites are packed on their own: `trainers.png` carries a
"Regular Trainers" grid of 64x64 sprites on flat orange 64px cells at a 65px
pitch, and `scripts/frlg/build-trainer-sprites.mjs` crops the ten the archetypes
use (keying the orange to transparent) into `src/assets/frlg/trainers.png` plus
`src/data/frlg/trainers.json`, read through `src/data/trainerSprites.ts` (D20).

The overworld sprites come from the pret/pokefirered decomp rather than
the compilation sheet, because each class is its own named file there. The ten
trainer classes' `graphics/object_events/pics/people/*.png` (mostly 160x32, ten
16x32 frames — nine walk frames plus a field-move pose the route ignores) are
packed by `scripts/frlg/build-overworld-sprites.mjs` into
`src/assets/frlg/overworld.png` (one row of nine poses per sprite key) plus
`src/data/frlg/overworld.json`, read through `src/data/overworldSprites.ts` (D24),
so a trainer stands on the resting pose of its facing and walks toward a player it
spots. The same atlas carries the slot machine's `rocket` grunt — an NPC of its own
since `Task-117` — and the ten special NPCs the rooms place — `merchant`, `chef`,
`magician`, `exorcist`, `weather-man`, `sacrifice`, `medic`, `iv-judge`,
`berry-tree`, `ninja` — for 21 rows in all. Six
of those borrow a shipped class because the decomp has no class by that name
(`magician` → gentleman, `exorcist` → channeler, `weather-man` → scientist,
`sacrifice` → old man, `iv-judge` → gym guide, `berry-tree` → hiker), and the
manifest's `fallbacks` records which; `medic` and `ninja` instead read the
decomp's own shorter strips (`nurse.png` 64x32, `koga.png` 48x32) through the
repetition map of its pose table. The packer resolves its source directory only
from argv or `PKM_FRLG_OVERWORLD_DIR`, as an absolute path outside the worktree,
per the asset-tree rule. The player comes from the same decomp:
`red_normal.png` (144x32, nine 16x32 walk frames) is packed by
`scripts/frlg/build-player-sprite.mjs` into `src/assets/frlg/player.png` plus
`src/data/frlg/player.json`, read through `src/data/playerSprite.ts`, so the
route draws the FRLG protagonist instead of the PMD placeholder. The
`npc-overworld.png` and `player-sprites.png` compilations stay unused.
The tall grass' rustle is the same decomp's
`graphics/field_effects/pics/tall_grass.png` (16x80, five 16x16 frames), packed by
`scripts/frlg/build-tilesets.mjs` into the three field atlases' unused cells, and
like every other rip it lives in `sprite-sources/frlg/` (gitignored, never
redistributed).

## Pokémon battle sprites (Pokémon Añil, local only)

The battle art is the one the Spanish fangame **Pokémon Añil** (EricLostie)
uses. It is a RPG Maker XP / Pokémon Essentials game, so its sheets live in
`Graphics/Pokemon/{Front,Back,Front shiny,Back shiny,Icons}` and each one is a
**horizontal filmstrip**: every frame is a square of the sheet's own height and
the frame count is `width / height`, the layout Añil's own *Pokémon Animados
para DBK* plugin reads. The sheets are 16-colour palette PNGs (~365 bytes a
frame), so they are copied verbatim into `public/anil/` — repacking them into
RGBA atlases would inflate the same art to gigabytes.

`scripts/anil/build-pokemon-sprites.mjs` is run against a checkout of the game
(`node scripts/anil/build-pokemon-sprites.mjs <anil>/Graphics/Pokemon`, or
`npm run data:anil` with `POKEANIL_POKEMON` set) and writes
`src/data/anil/pokemon-sprites.json`, read through `src/data/pokemonSprites.ts`
(battle) and `src/data/portraits.ts` (HUD/party). A dex species resolves to
`<SPECIES>.png` (with `_female`, and the shiny folders for the alternate
palette) by normalised name.

A **forme** resolves to `<SPECIES>_<n>.png`, where `n` is its position in our
dex, but only when the Añil set lists exactly `1..n` for that species. The ones
that do not line up live in the script's `FORM_OVERRIDE` table, keyed by dex id:
the Alcremie cream/sweet grid, Arceus by type, the cap Pikachu, Minior's cores,
Ogerpon's masks, Kyurem's black/white swap, Greninja, Basculin, Rockruff, and
the Vivillon patterns, which use the **official Spanish** pattern names (Icy
Snow is "Polar", Polar is "Taiga", High Plains is "Estepa", River is "Oasis",
Savanna is "Pantano"...). A forme with no confident match falls back to its base
species rather than drawing the wrong mon.

The same script packs the **portraits**: one 64x64 cell per dex entry, in dex
order, from the `Icons` folders, into `public/anil/portraits.png` and
`portraits-shiny.png` (the icons are 64px, so the atlas is under half a megabyte
each). The battle's switch grid, the route HUD, the starter cards and the
release beat draw from it.

Item icons come from Añil as well: `sprite-sources/anil/Graphics/Items/` holds
893 icons (48x48, except 44 at 50-60 px) whose file names are the `[ID]` headers
of `PBS/items.txt`. `scripts/anil/build-item-icons.mjs` (`npm run data:anil:items`)
packs each of them into a 24px frame — the 48x48 sources sampled 2:1, the 50-60 px
ones scaled per axis — into the gitignored
`src/assets/anil/item-icons.png` (8 columns, nearest-neighbour) plus
`src/data/anil/item-icons.json`, read through `src/data/itemIcons.ts`. The
manifest is keyed by the **normalised** file name, so a catalogue id that matches
an Añil file gets its own art with no table to keep; a TM draws the machine of
the move it teaches, so each machine on sale shows its own type's art, and the
Normal machine stays the fallback for a TM whose move is unknown (the atlas
already carries Añil's `machine_<TYPE>.png` set). The FRLG item-icons sheet
(`src/assets/frlg/item-icons.png`) and its packer stay in the tree, unused.

Never redistributed: keep the checkout out of the repo (`sprite-sources/` is
gitignored). Credits, as the game itself prints them in
`Settings.game_credits`:

| What | Who |

| Battle sprites (Gen 4/5/DS style) | TheLuiz, leparagon, French-Cyndaquil, Z-nogyroP, Prodigal96, Vanilla Sunshine, fishbowlsoul90, zlolxd, elazulmax, mangamanga, MrDollSteak, Spherical-Ice, Dreadwing93, the **Smogon Gen 8 Sprite Project**, the **Smogon Sun/Moon Sprite Project** and the **Gen VI: Pokémon Sprite Resource** |

## FireRed/LeafGreen UI sources (downloaded, local only)

The interface is moving to FRLG art (menus, effects, HUD). The sheets live in
`sprite-sources/frlg-ui/` (gitignored, never redistributed), downloaded from
spriters-resource [`game_boy_advance/pokemonfireredleafgreen`](https://www.spriters-resource.com/game_boy_advance/pokemonfireredleafgreen/):

| File | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `menu-frames.png` | text/menu window frames |
| `hpbars-menu.png` | HP bars and the in-battle command menu |
| `interface-bag.png`, `pokemon-menu.png`, `summary-menu.png` | bag, party and summary screens |
| `type-status-icons.png` | type/status badges |
| `fonts.png` | the FRLG glyph set |
| `attack-effects.png`, `move-effects.png`, `stat-effects.png`, `battle-backgrounds.png` | battle FX and backdrops |

The route's **ground-item** sprite is the standalone Poke Ball that sits above the
right corner of the Pokemon Center on the Hoeloo compilation
(`sprite-sources/frlg/tileset.png`); `scripts/frlg/build-field-items.mjs` crops it
to 16x16 and keys the flat background into the gitignored
`src/assets/frlg/ground-item.png`, read through `src/data/groundItemSprite.ts`.
Hidden items draw the procedural sparkle (`BootScene`) instead.

The HP readout and the command cursor still come from the same sheet:
`scripts/frlg/build-battle-ui.mjs` crops and 4x-upscales the HP readout — the
"HP" chip plus an empty track, `src/assets/frlg/hpbar.png` — and the ▶ command
cursor (`src/assets/frlg/cursor.png`) for the 1280x800 canvas, drawn by
`src/ui/frlg.ts`. The measured geometry (the 4x scale and the HP track's fill
rectangle) is generated alongside them in `src/data/frlg/battle-ui.json`, so the
scene never hardcodes pixel maths. The scene paints the current HP colour behind
the bar's overlay. The same builder still writes the light menu window
(`frlg/window.png`) and the message box (`frlg/textbox.png`), which the drawn
set below supersedes and nothing reads.

The **window chrome is drawn, not ripped**: `scripts/ui/build-chrome.mjs`
(`npm run data:ui`) paints `src/assets/ui/window.png` (the menu card: panels,
lists, menus) and `src/assets/ui/textbox.png` (the battle's message box) with the
repo's own pixel codec, hard pixels and no antialiasing. Each keeps the FRLG
frame's outer shape and baked content insets, so no scene's layout moved, and
each is a nine-slice whose corner is **11** — the packed corner *is* the runtime
radius, since `addFrlgWindow` hands `chrome.window.corner` straight to
`setSlices` and the atlas is drawn 1:1 — with its frame band drawn inside that
corner, because a 128px texture is drawn at any panel size and a band wider than
the corner would stretch with the panel, while the frozen 36/28/28 padding stays
the content inset inside the battle box's field. The corner and the padding are
exported as `FRLG_WINDOW_PADDING` / `FRLG_TEXTBOX_PADDING` and driven by
`src/data/ui/chrome.json`. The two skins are the **same dark glass card**: the
`#10161f` surface, a 1px hairline ring just inside the card's own edge, a 1px
shadow ring on that edge, an inset highlight row at `y = 11` and the `tokens`
block (the surface, the ink `#f8fafc`, the muted `#94a3b8`, the accent `#7dd3fc`,
the badge ink, the semantic pair and the row / selected / track tones) written
into `chrome.json`, which `src/ui/frlg.ts` exports typed. Every ring tone is
written **pre-composited over the surface**, because the skin carries the surface
as its field and `addFrlgWindow` applies the skin's own translucency once — 0.72
for a menu card, 0.80 for the battle box — so the route and the battle stay
readable through the panels with every scene inheriting it; the PNGs stay opaque
so a scene that fades a window multiplies cleanly instead of double-applying. The
FRLG `window.png`/`textbox.png` stay in the tree as the source this derives from,
unused.

The bitmap font is packed the same way: `scripts/frlg/build-font.mjs` reads
`latin_normal.png` + `charmap.txt`, keys the sheet's flat blue background and
white padding to transparent, keeps the letter white with a mid-grey shadow (so
a tint recolours it for light or dark windows) and 2x-upscales it into
`src/assets/frlg/font.png` plus the per-glyph metrics in
`src/data/frlg/font.json`, read at runtime by `src/ui/frlgFont.ts`
(`addFrlgText`). Only the Latin glyphs up to the Japanese sections are packed,
plus the four named arrows.

The face the interface actually draws with is the **derived** one:
`scripts/ui/build-chrome.mjs` rewrites the committed atlas' shadow pixels from
opaque mid grey to a **half-alpha black halo** — a glyph reads as a soft halo on
the dark glass instead of a solid second tone — keeps the ink pure white so one
texture serves every string the scenes tint, and copies every metric
byte-for-byte into `src/assets/ui/font.png` + `src/data/ui/font.json` (`name`
`UI`), read through `src/ui/frlgFont.ts`. Text on the card is the light
`#f8fafc` (`FRLG_INK`, read from `chrome.json`'s `tokens.ink`), on a full-colour
type badge or chip the dark `FRLG_BADGE_INK`. The glyph shapes are the rip's: a
different typeface would need a licensed/open font asset plus a rasteriser, which
is a task of its own.

The **type badges** now come from Pokémon Añil rather than this FRLG sheet: the
fangame's `Graphics/UI/types.png` is one column of 19 badges — the 18 types in dex
order with the "???" slot between Steel and Fire, then Fairy — that
`scripts/frlg/build-type-icons.mjs` nearest-neighbour scales into the frozen 32x14
frames of `src/assets/frlg/type-icons.png`, with the frame map in
`src/data/frlg/type-icons.json`, read through `src/data/typeIcons.ts`. The packer
takes the sheet path (`node scripts/frlg/build-type-icons.mjs
<anil>/Graphics/UI/types.png`, or `npm run data:frlg:icons`). Every type the dex
lists has a badge now, Fairy included, so the text-chip fallback is dead for real
types. Añil's "???" slot is a flat blue placeholder with no glyph; the `unknown`
frame is packed but nothing draws it.

The **status badges** (SLP / PSN / BRN / PAR / FRZ, plus the FNT and PKRS spares)
come from the same fangame's `Graphics/UI/statuses.png`, one column of seven 44x16
badges (DOR / ENV / QUE / PAR / CON / DEB / PKRS).
`scripts/frlg/build-status-icons.mjs` nearest-neighbour scales them into
`src/assets/frlg/status-icons.png` (32x8 frames) with the map in
`src/data/frlg/status-icons.json`, read through `src/data/statusIcons.ts`. Añil's UI
sheet ships no separate badly-poisoned badge, so `tox` keeps sharing the poison
frame, and the battle HP panel draws the badge in place of the text abbreviation.

## Pokémon overworld sprites (PMDCollab, local only)

The overworld Pokémon art is **PMDCollab's SpriteCollab** project
(`github.com/PMDCollab/SpriteCollab`, served by `spriteserver.pmdcollab.org`):
top-down Mystery Dungeon dungeon sprites for Pokémon of every generation, one
pack per species and per forme at `assets/<NNNN>/sprites.zip` (a forme is
`assets/<NNNN>/<FFFF>/sprites.zip`, `FFFF` SpriteCollab's own 4-digit form
index — Castform-Sunny is `0351/0001`). Each pack carries one PNG per animation
plus an `AnimData.xml` with the exact frame size, frame count and per-frame tick
durations, so no coordinate is hand-eyeballed and no chroma-keying is needed
(the sheets already have a real alpha channel).

The pipeline is the three dependency-free tools under `scripts/legacy-art/`, and
its **work list is derived from our dex**, never hand-written:

| Step | Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `fetch-pmdcollab.mjs` | downloads a pack (or `--table`, every row of `pmdcollab-species.mjs`; `--missing` only the rows with no local pack) into `<dest>/<slug>-pmdcollab/` |
| 2 | `extract-real-sprites.mjs` | crops, trims, scales and repacks the frames into the gitignored `src/assets/pokemon/<fileId>.png` + committed `.json` pair (`--only-missing` skips a pair that is already packed) |
| 3 | `build-pokemon-index.mjs` | writes the committed coverage manifest `src/assets/pokemon/index.json` from those pairs and `src/data/dex/species.json` |

`--dest` and `--source` are **absolute** paths, required, with `PMDCOLLAB_SOURCES`
as the environment fallback; no tool defaults to a path inside the repo, because a
source tree under `sprite-sources/` is never junctioned or symlinked into a
worktree (AGENTS.md's "Asset trees" rule). `scripts/legacy-art/pmdcollab-species.mjs`
is the table the three share: one row per playable species PMDCollab has art for,
in dex order, giving the dex id, the dex number, the SpriteCollab form index, the
local folder slug and the download path.

The pair's file id is `num` for a base species and `num * 100 + formIndex` for a
forme, so Castform's three Forecast formes are `35101`, `35102` and `35103`. A
species PMDCollab has no pack for (the Totem formes, Minior-Meteor,
Sinistea/Polteageist-Antique, Basculegion-F, Oinkologne-F, Maushold-Four,
Rockruff-Dusk, Greninja-Bond) is listed in the manifest's `missing` array instead
and a reader draws nothing for it, as is one whose pack carries no usable
Walk/Attack/Hurt block (Jellicent, Stonjourner, Iron Treads...). A pack with no
`Shoot` anim gets its special slot from the row's `shootAnim` override
(`SpAttack`, or `Charge` where there is no `SpAttack`), so the special block is
never left empty.

Credit, as the repository itself prints it: the SpriteCollab contributors. Its
custom graphics are licensed **CC BY-NC 4.0**; the art derived from the official
games is Nintendo/GameFreak/Chunsoft's. Like every other rip here it is local
development art only — `sprite-sources/` is gitignored and the packed sheets must
not be redistributed.

## Sprite contract

- Overworld Pokémon sheets: 32x32 frames, 5 rows in the order Down, DownRight,
  Up, UpRight, Left; columns are concatenated animation frames. One pair per
  species and per forme, `<fileId>.png` + `<fileId>.json` side by side, named
  after the dex number (or `num * 100 + form index` for a forme). The JSON
  manifest carries `cols` and the five animation blocks (`idle`, `walk`,
  `attack`, `special`, `hurt`) with `start`, `count`, `durationsMs` and, where
  the source declares one, `hitFrame` — `start` and `count` are row-0 column
  offsets, so the frame at step `i` of an animation in direction row `r` is
  `r * cols + start + i`. `src/assets/pokemon/index.json` is the coverage
  manifest a reader resolves a species through: `files` maps a species id to its
  pair, `missing` lists the ids PMDCollab has no art for. Packed from PMDCollab
  by `scripts/legacy-art/` (see the PMD Collab section above).
- Trainer overworld sprites: one 16x32 frame per archetype, the down-facing idle
  from the FRLG decomp sheet, drawn at `TILE_SIZE / 16` with its feet on the tile
  bottom; the tinted arrow still shows facing (`D24`).
- Player overworld sprite: the resting pose plus a two-frame walk cycle for each
  of down, up and the left-facing side (right is the side frame flipped), from
  the FRLG decomp `red_normal.png` via `scripts/frlg/build-player-sprite.mjs`.
  The route draws the 16x32 frame at `TILE_SIZE / 16` with its feet on the tile
  bottom, plays the direction's walk cycle while a step slides and stands on the
  resting frame when it stops; the run key scales the cycle's rate up (`D25`).
- Sheets are loaded lazily per species so a route only pays for what it shows.
- Portraits: `public/anil/portraits.png` and `portraits-shiny.png` are 32-column
  grids of 64x64 cells, one per dex entry in dex order, packed from Pokémon
  Añil's `Icons` sheets by `scripts/anil/build-pokemon-sprites.mjs`
  (`src/data/portraits.ts`). A member's shiny flag picks the shiny atlas, and the
  HUD, the battle's switch grid, the starter cards and the release beat all draw
  from it.

## Rules

- Prefer generating or composing from what is already in `src/assets` over adding
  a new dependency or a new source pack.
- Any new sheet keeps the atlas contract (`docs/tileset-spec.md`) so it can be
  swapped without code.
- Routes and dungeons match the original FireRed/LeafGreen look for their biome
  (Viridian Forest, Mt. Moon, Seafoam...): the generator follows the tileset's
  style, not a generic room layout (`docs/routes-spec.md` §2).
- Chalk-drawn UI is not a thing here; this project's UI is the classic Pokémon
  frame, now the drawn chrome (`src/assets/ui/**` through `addFrlgWindow`) with
  the derived bitmap glyph set (`ui/font.png` through `addFrlgText`), so menus,
  HUD labels and battle messages are all bitmap text instead of a system font
  and the interface ships (`O4`).
- **Sound is the same three tiers as art.** The whole vocabulary —
  `src/assets/audio/<cue-id>.wav`, three music beds, the one-shot cues, the shared
  `hit` and one cue per move sound and per cry — is imported FRLG audio
  (local-only, gitignored at the source: `sprite-sources/frlg-audio/`), ripped by
  `scripts/frlg/render-frlg-audio.mjs` and packed by `scripts/frlg/build-audio.mjs`
  (`D22` addendum). No cue is hand-edited and no scene names a file: a CC0 set is
  a re-run of the packer, and a cue no longer wanted is a row dropped from the
  table, not an asset deleted from a `preload`. A generated procedural placeholder
  exists only for a *hand row* whose rip is missing; a cue of a ripped family is
  real audio or a hard error.
